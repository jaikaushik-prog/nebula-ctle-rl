## 1. What was generated, and by what

**Not by hand, and not by RL.** The benchmark's P3 rung scores every proposal on
the worst of 3 screen corners × 2 loads. Its `uniform` arm found two designs
that cleared every spec at all six points, in 20 seeds — rewards **8.0342** and
**8.0021**, against a feasibility bonus of exactly 8.0. Both are marginal by
construction: they are the designs that only just qualify.

Their sizing vectors are read out of the run log by
`exp_g4_verify.candidates()`, so no number in this document was transcribed by
a human.

**Stated plainly for the report: the corner-robust design was found by uniform
random search, not by the RL policy.** G4's criterion says "generated", not
"generated by RL". `rl/corner_env.py` (session 22k) now makes worst-corner
scoring available *inside* the RL loop, and has not yet been run at scale.

## 3. The design that failed, and why it matters more

`c9d52866743dc1`, from `uniform` replicate 16, claimed **8.0021** at the screen.
**8 of 135 points fail.**

| corner | load | reward | verdict | binding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ss/1.05/125 °C` | 78.0 fF | **−10.0000** | **invalid** | — |
| `sf/1.05/125 °C` | 78.0 fF | **−10.0000** | **invalid** | — |
| `sf/0.95/0 °C` | 13.6 fF | −0.0121 | valid | `S3_f_peak` |
| `sf/1.00/0 °C` | 13.6 fF | −0.0171 | valid | `S3_f_peak` |
| `sf/1.05/0 °C` | 13.6 fF | −0.0237 | valid | `S3_f_peak` |
| `fs/0.95/125 °C` | 78.0 fF | −0.0104 | valid | `S3_f_peak` |
| `fs/1.00/125 °C` | 78.0 fF | −0.0203 | valid | `S3_f_peak` |
| `fs/1.05/125 °C` | 78.0 fF | −0.0210 | valid | `S3_f_peak` |

**None of the eight is a screened corner.** The screen is
`ss/0.95/125 °C`, `ff/1.05/0 °C`, `ss/0.95/0 °C` — all-slow and all-fast. Every
failure is at a **mixed** corner, `sf` or `fs`, of which the screen has no
member, plus one all-slow point at the *high* supply the screen does not carry.

The mechanism is legible: the mixed corners skew the NMOS/PMOS balance, which
moves the bias point and therefore `gm`, and `f_peak` follows. Neither all-slow
nor all-fast reproduces that skew, so the screen is blind to it by construction
rather than by bad luck. Six of the eight failures are `S3_f_peak` misses of
**0.01–0.02 octaves** — the design is a hair outside the window, at corners
nothing looked at.

## 4. The control, which is what makes this a gate

A verification that only runs on designs expected to pass cannot fail (G73). So
the same 135 points ran on the **three strongest TT-only designs** the
benchmark produced — 8.9995, 8.9993, 8.9993, near the reward ceiling, feasible
at nominal and never certified at any corner.

| design | claimed at TT | corner points failed |
|---|---|---|
| `6484611d7a84` | 8.9995 | **75 of 135** |
| `6bf71903821b` | 8.9993 | **22 of 135** |
| `f4829115423c` | 8.9993 | **9 of 135** |

All three fail. **The gate can fail, and it was demonstrated rather than
asserted.** And the comparison is the argument for contribution #1 in one line:
*the best design at nominal fails 75 of 135 corner points; a design scored on
its worst corner from the start passes all of them.*

## 6. G99 — a screen calibrated on the POPULATION is not a screen for its own SURVIVORS

G47 measured that the three screen corners capture **98.7 %** of what all 45
catch. That number is a statistic over **the whole box**, where most designs
fail obviously and any corner catches them.

**Survivors are the opposite population.** They are, by construction, the
designs sitting on the boundary — both of ours scored within 0.035 of the
feasibility bonus. On the boundary, a 1.3 % blind spot is not rare: **1 of our
2 survivors failed, and 100 % of its failures were in the blind spot.**

> **A screen's accuracy on the population it was calibrated on says nothing
> about its accuracy on the designs it lets through.** Measure a screen on its
> own output, not on the box.

Recorded as **G99** in `HANDOFF.md` §9. The practical consequence: the screen
stays as a *search* device — it is 7.5× cheaper than the full grid and it did
put both survivors within reach — but **no design may be called corner-robust
on the screen alone.** Verification is the full grid, and it costs 135
simulations, which is under 15 seconds.

## 7. What is still open

* **The corner-aware RL loop has not been run at scale.** `rl/corner_env.py`
  exists and is tested; the design verified here came from uniform random
  search. Running PPO on the corner-aware objective is the next experiment and
  needs `baselines.method_ppo`'s structural refusal lifted.
* **Should the screen gain a mixed corner?** `sf` and `fs` are where both
  survivors were tightest. Adding one would raise the search cost from 3
  corners to 4 (+33 %) and is a change to the benchmark, so it is an owner's
  decision (`CONTINUE_HERE.md` §5).
* **Two failures were `invalid`, not merely out of spec** — non-convergence or
  an unusable measurement at `ss/1.05/125 °C` and `sf/1.05/125 °C`, both at the
  heavy load. Whether that is a real circuit failure or a simulator failure is
  unmeasured, and G26/G30 say an exit code will not tell you.
* **S8 (the eye) is not in this verification.** `reward_v1` scores `V1_SPECS`,
  which excludes S8; the bridge exists (`G2_RESULTS.md`) but wiring it into the
  corner sweep is a separate re-run event.
